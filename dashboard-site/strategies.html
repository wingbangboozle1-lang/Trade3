Simulated strategies

The point of the simulator is not to look profitable. It is to find out which kind of trade is worth doing on this venue, so every strategy here has an explicit thesis, an explicit entry rule and an explicit exit rule — and the results are reported per strategy type rather than as one blended number.

Nothing here places a real order. Trades are simulated in a local ledger starting at $1,000 of paper cash, with one position per market to keep experiments independent. Reading the real demo portfolio is a separate, read-only feature that needs your API key.

The four strategies

How a trade is routed

  1. An alert fires only when a contract moves at least $0.10.
  2. The alert is scored. Only alerts scoring 45 or higher reach the high-ROI feed, and only those are eligible to open a paper position.
  3. The router picks a strategy deterministically, so replaying the same alert always produces the same trade — that reproducibility is what makes the comparison meaningful.
  4. Position size is the smaller of the strategy's default and 5% of available paper cash. Contracts are whole numbers sized against the entry price.
  5. Prices within 2 cents of $0.00 or $1.00 are skipped: there is no room left for a payoff to be worth the modelled cost.

Cost model used in the score

Each candidate trade carries an explicit cost per contract: half the bid-ask spread plus Kalshi's published fee, M × 0.07 × C × P × (1 − P), where M is the series' own fee_multiplier. Those two numbers, and the resulting net edge, are printed on every high-ROI alert.

Fee formula source: Kalshi fee schedule, effective 2026-07-07. The schedule also states there is no settlement fee and no membership fee.

Results so far

This is the offline verification harness result, not a live session. Run npm start with normal network access and the same table fills from live demo alerts. Empty is an acceptable, honest answer: on the demo environment many markets sit at $0.00, so qualifying swings are rare.

Exit rules

Reading the results honestly

  • Small samples lie. A 70% win rate on 10 trades is noise, not edge.
  • Demo prices are not real. Kalshi warns the demo “may not be reflective of those in real markets”, so a strategy that wins here may not survive production liquidity.
  • Fills are optimistic. The simulator assumes you transact at the modelled price. Real resting orders may not fill at all.
  • One position per market avoids stacking correlated risk, but it also hides how the strategy scales.
