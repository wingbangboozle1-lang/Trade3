Executive summary

What this is. A working, documented system for watching Kalshi's demo exchange, limited to five topics (gas prices, elections, culture, crypto, golf), that flags every contract-price move of $0.10 or more in either direction, publishes those flags to two live scrolling feeds, follows each market to its YES/NO resolution, and runs four simulated strategies against the same signals so their results can be compared rather than assumed. Everything it claims about Kalshi is traceable to a Kalshi-owned source; see Sources.

What it is not. It is not a live-trading bot, and it is not connected to your demo account in this repository (no key can be committed, and should not be). The order path is implemented against the official V2 contract and is exercised against a documented mock; it activates only when you supply a demo key at runtime.

The model, in one paragraph

A sharp move makes one side expensive. Buying that side back as a taker costs the ask and earns the mid, so it loses half the spread plus the taker fee every time — that is arithmetic, not a market view. Resting a bid on the side that got cheaper earns the half-spread instead, and at a 0× maker multiplier it keeps it. The model therefore scores the maker view, requires the move to be at least twice the observed spread (so the move is signal, not noise), requires at least 1.5% expected edge on capital, requires two-sided depth of at least 25 contracts, and refuses anything closing inside a minute. Every promotion prints its inputs. The measured cost of this view — adverse selection, i.e. fills that cluster when the market is already moving against you — is tracked separately and reported rather than averaged away.

Bottom line

  • The alerting requirement is met exactly as specified: ≥$0.10, either direction, two

scrolling feeds, one of them filtered by a scored, auditable model.

  • The resolution requirement is met from the only live source that carries it

(market_lifecycle_v2).

  • The simulated trading requirement is met with four strategies and a per-category

analytics breakdown; the maker/taker comparison is the headline result and it is a caution, not a promise.

  • The verification requirement is met structurally: every table row on this site carries

a source ID that resolves to a Kalshi-owned URL, and a build step fails if a row is published without one. Unverifiable values are blank and appear on the Flagged for review page.

  • The live dashboard runs from app/ (npm start). It cannot be a static GitHub Pages

page, because a static page cannot hold a private key or keep a WebSocket open. That constraint is stated on the site rather than worked around with fake data.
